## Question

A historian could best explain the arguments made in the passage regarding the pope and the clergy in the context of Protestant claims that the Catholic Church

## Answer Choices

- A. had not adequately supported earlier Crusades against Muslims in the Holy Land
- B. had become corrupted by power
- C. had become too heavily influenced by Renaissance Humanism
- D. had failed to convert Muslims living in Europe to Christianity

## Answer

> [!answer]- Reveal Answer
> **Correct Answer: B**
>
> ### Rationale
>
> Luther accuses the papal and episcopal hierarchy of hypocrisy: it claims to represent Christ, yet it calls for wars fought in Christ's name against the Turks — and, he observes, the bishops' own presence has not saved Christian armies from heavy defeat at Varna. A historical reading places this in the context of the Protestant charge that the Catholic Church had become morally and institutionally corrupted by its wealth, its political power, and its involvement in worldly affairs — the sale of indulgences, the political manoeuvring of popes, and here, the clergy's role in warfare.
>
> **Why the other choices are wrong**
>
> - **A.** Protestants did not argue that the Church had failed to support the Crusades; Luther's point is nearly the reverse — that crusading itself contradicted Christ. And by 1528 the Crusades had been over for well over a century, so the criticism is anachronistic.
> - **C.** Renaissance humanism had no substantial role in Luther's indictment. He makes no appeal to classical learning, textual criticism, or classical models, and humanism is not the framework of this argument.
> - **D.** Luther is arguing with Muslims as enemies of Christians, not lamenting that the Church failed to convert Europe's Muslims. Nothing about unsuccessful conversion appears in the passage.
