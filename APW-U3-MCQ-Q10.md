## Question

Which of the following developments in the period 1450–1750 could best be used as evidence to modify Darwin’s argument in the first paragraph about the establishment of Eurasian empires on “nomadic foundations”?

## Answer Choices

- A. The creation of the Ottoman Empire
- B. The Ming overthrow of the Yuan dynasty in China
- C. The fall of the Mughal Empire
- D. The establishment of the Safavid Empire

## Answer

> [!answer]- Reveal Answer
> **Correct Answer: A**
>
> ### Rationale
>
> Darwin's first paragraph claims that by Tamerlane's time it was no longer possible to build a Eurasian empire on nomadic foundations. The Ottoman Empire is the development that qualifies this. Founded by Oghuz Turks on the Anatolian frontier and expanding steadily for two centuries, it became the most durable of the gunpowder empires — and it did so by converting its nomadic military inheritance into a settled, bureaucratic, territorially continuous state with an elaborate apparatus of court, tax, and professional troops. Its survival forces a revision: nomadic origin had not become impossible as a route to empire.
>
> **Why the other choices are wrong**
>
> - **B.** The Ming overthrow of the Yuan dynasty in 1368 shows the reverse: that settled Chinese dynasties could expel a Mongol dynasty. It supports Darwin's argument rather than modifying it.
> - **C.** The Mughal Empire's decline in the eighteenth century is very much what Darwin describes — the failure of the nomadic-foundation model. It corroborates him.
> - **D.** The Safavid Empire was a durable gunpowder state, but it was founded by a Shi'a dynasty in Persian lands rather than by a nomadic conquest empire of the Tamerlane type, and its persistence does not complicate the point about nomadic foundations in the way the Ottoman case does.
