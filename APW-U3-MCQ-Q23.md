## Question

Outside of the Ottoman Empire, Sufis contributed most directly to which of the following during the period before 1750?

## Answer Choices

- A. Scientific exchanges between the Muslim world and the rest of Afro-Eurasia
- B. The establishment of Arabic as the language of philosophy and theology in the Muslim world
- C. The spread of Islam to new locations on the margins of the Muslim world, such as southeast Asia
- D. The introduction of new practices for recruiting and training slave soldiers in Muslim states, such as the Mughal Empire

## Answer

> [!answer]- Reveal Answer
> **Correct Answer: C**
>
> ### Rationale
>
> Sufi teachers — saints, preachers, and holy men who travelled and settled at the edges of the Islamic world — were the principal agents of Islam's expansion beyond its original Arab and Persian heartlands. Their work carried Islam into Bengal, the Maldives, the Malay-Indonesian archipelago, and parts of Central Africa: places where a Sufi lodge, a saint's tomb, or a converted ruler's court anchored a new Muslim community. This was a distinct contribution from that of state-sponsored conquest, and it operated well beyond Ottoman territory.
>
> **Why the other choices are wrong**
>
> - **A.** Scientific exchange between the Muslim world and the rest of Afro-Eurasia was carried through court patronage, translation movements, and merchant and scholarly networks — the tradition of the House of Wisdom and its successors — not primarily through Sufi orders.
> - **B.** Arabic had already been the language of theology and philosophy in the Muslim world for centuries, long before the period in question, and Sufis did not establish it. They generally worked in vernacular languages.
> - **D.** Training slave and military contingents was a state practice of the Ottoman, Safavid, and Mughal systems. Sufi orders were religious communities, and they did not design recruitment or training programmes for imperial armies.
