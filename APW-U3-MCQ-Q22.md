## Question

Which of the following most directly strengthened Sunni religious scholars’ role as official interpreters of Islamic doctrine within the Ottoman Empire, as suggested by the passage?

## Answer Choices

- A. The establishment of the Mughal Empire in India
- B. The Ottoman conquest of Constantinople
- C. Ottoman sultans’ extensive conquests in Europe
- D. The Ottoman Empire’s rivalry with the Safavid Empire

## Answer

> [!answer]- Reveal Answer
> **Correct Answer: B**
>
> ### Rationale
>
> The Sunni scholarly establishment's standing in the Ottoman Empire rested on the empire's possession of the major centres of Sunni Islam. The conquest of Constantinople in 1453 made the sultan heir to the Byzantine capital and, shortly afterward, brought under Ottoman rule much of the Arab Middle East and Egypt, whose populations were overwhelmingly Sunni and whose scholars were the most numerous and institutionally established in the Islamic world. As Ottoman jurisdiction expanded over Sunni populations, the ulema gained the courts, endowed mosques and madrasas, the qadi system, and the imperial apparatus of law and doctrine — and with it the authority to adjudicate practice against popular Sufi religion as the passage describes.
>
> **Why the other choices are wrong**
>
> - **A.** The Mughal Empire lay far to the east and had no bearing on the Ottoman Empire's internal balance between the Sunni ulema and Sufi orders.
> - **C.** Ottoman conquests in Europe extended the empire over Christian populations, who were administered through the millet system and had no bearing on Sunni–Sufi disputes inside the Muslim population.
> - **D.** Safavid–Ottoman rivalry was confessional — Shi'a against Sunni — and did produce heightened Sunni identity in the Ottoman lands. But the choice is indirect and the passage concerns the institutional standing of Sunni scholars, which rested on the expansion of Ottoman rule over the Sunni centres of the Arab world.
