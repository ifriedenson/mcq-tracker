## Stimulus

![[U3-MCQ-S04-img1.png]]

> WALL PAINTING FROM THE PALACE OF THE RULERS OF THE SOUTHERN INDIAN STATE OF
>
> GOLKONDA, CIRCA 1650

## Question

The particular event depicted in the painting was likely important to Golkonda rulers mostly because it

## Answer Choices

- A. could be used as a symbol of the union between the state’s ruling class and the majority of its population
- B. provided a source of pride for Golkonda’s Muslim population by reminding them of the Muslim conquests of India
- C. led to the decline of patriarchal social structures in the Indian subcontinent
- D. illustrated the extent to which Golkonda elites relied on various forms of coerced labor

## Answer

> [!answer]- Reveal Answer
> **Correct Answer: A**
>
> ### Rationale
>
> Golconda was a small Muslim-ruled state in a Deccan society that was overwhelmingly Hindu. A wedding uniting the Muslim ruler to a Hindu bride, painted on the palace wall for the court to see, functioned as a symbolic claim that the dynasty belonged to the whole of its population. Such imagery let a small Muslim elite present itself as legitimate to a Hindu majority, and it advertised that Hindu elites were honoured partners rather than subjects.
>
> **Why the other choices are wrong**
>
> - **B.** Nothing in the scene evokes the Muslim conquests of India, and the painting dates to about 1650 — long before any such conquest by Golconda, which was itself conquered in 1687. A royal marriage is not a reminder of conquests.
> - **C.** One royal wedding could not have altered patriarchal structures across the Indian subcontinent. Kinship and gender arrangements in India remained strongly patrilineal throughout this period.
> - **D.** The painting says nothing about labour. Attendants appear at a royal ceremony, but the image offers no evidence about coerced labour or the economic arrangements behind the court.
