## Question

Which of the following developments in the late fifteenth century could best be used as evidence to support Darwin’s argument in the second paragraph regarding a change in patterns of long-distance trade?

## Answer Choices

- A. The growing naval power of the Ottoman Empire in the Mediterranean
- B. The discovery of maritime routes that linked Europe and Asia through the Indian Ocean
- C. The naval expeditions of the Ming admiral Zheng He
- D. The development of the Atlantic system that linked Europe to the Americas and Africa

## Answer

> [!answer]- Reveal Answer
> **Correct Answer: B**
>
> ### Rationale
>
> Darwin argues that within decades of Tamerlane's death the overland Silk Road corridor he had fought to control was losing its centrality. The development that best evidences this is the opening of direct maritime routes between Europe and Asia: Vasco da Gama reached Calicut in 1498, and subsequent voyages established a sea lane around Africa to India and the Indian Ocean. From that point European commerce with Asia could bypass Central Asia entirely, and the caravan cities of the Silk Road declined relative to the new oceanic ports.
>
> **Why the other choices are wrong**
>
> - **A.** Ottoman naval power in the Mediterranean is a regional matter and did not reroute the Asia trade that flowed overland through Samarkand and Bukhara. Ottoman naval growth did not redirect Eurasian long-distance commerce away from Central Asia.
> - **C.** Zheng He's expeditions (1405–1433) were state-sponsored tribute voyages that did not open a commercial route or create lasting maritime trade. They carried goods out to polite rivals rather than supplying a sustained alternative to overland trade.
> - **D.** The Atlantic system developed later and connected the Americas with Europe and Africa. It concerns the western hemisphere rather than the Europe–Asia corridor whose overland route Darwin is discussing.
