## Question

Which of the following statements is true about both the Mughal and the Ottoman empires in the sixteenth century?

## Answer Choices

- A. In each, the majority of the people were Muslims.
- B. Each had a powerful navy that engaged European navies.
- C. Each had developed an efficient administrative structure.
- D. Each enjoyed peaceful relations with its neighboring states.
- E. Each gave little monetary support to artistic and cultural endeavors.

## Answer

> [!answer]- Reveal Answer
> **Correct Answer: C**
>
> ### Rationale
>
> Both empires ran sophisticated administrative systems. The Ottomans assessed and recorded taxable property through periodic surveys, collected revenue through provincial officials, and staffed the army and administration with trained palace slaves and salaried officials. The Mughals did the same through the zabt and dasht system of land revenue assessment, the mansab-dawar hierarchy, and the mansabdari army whose officers were assigned revenue rather than paid in cash. Efficiency in governing a large, diverse, land-based empire was the shared institution.
>
> **Why the other choices are wrong**
>
> - **A.** False for both empires. The Mughal subjects were mostly Hindu and the Ottoman subjects included large non-Muslim majorities, so Muslims were not the majority of either people.
> - **B.** False for both. Neither empire built a naval force capable of engaging European navies; their power was land-based.
> - **D.** False for both. Both empires were frequently at war with their neighbours — the Ottomans with Safavids, Habsburgs, and Mamluks; the Mughals with Rajputs, Marathas, and Safavids.
> - **E.** False for both. Both courts spent heavily on the arts and on monumental building; the Ottomans on Sinan's imperial mosques and the Mughals on the Taj Mahal and Fatehpur Sikri.
