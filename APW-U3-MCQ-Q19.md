## Question

The Ottoman Empire’s recruitment of soldiers and bureaucrats through the system depicted in the image is most similar to which broader method that rulers used to strengthen their empires in the period 1450–1750 ?

## Answer Choices

- A. The collection of tribute
- B. Establishment of religious uniformity
- C. Abolition of feudal privileges
- D. The granting of autonomy to minority groups

## Answer

> [!answer]- Reveal Answer
> **Correct Answer: A**
>
> ### Rationale
>
> The devşirme system required the empire's Christian peasant communities to surrender a proportion of their boys, who were then raised in the palace schools and staffed into the army and bureaucracy without regard to their origin. Structurally this is an imperial levy — a claim on the resources of subject populations. Tribute collection across the Ottoman Balkans and Anatolia worked the same way, and other states did the same, including the Spanish crown's assessment of encomienda labour and the Ming system's registers. In each case an empire extracted a quota from communities it did not fully absorb.
>
> **Why the other choices are wrong**
>
> - **B.** Establishing religious uniformity was the opposite of what devşirme did. The Ottoman system deliberately recruited from non-Muslim subjects and rose above their communal identity, which is why Janissary officers could be Christians' sons while serving a Muslim state.
> - **C.** Abolition of feudal privileges belongs to later centralizing and revolutionary programmes in Europe and Japan, and the devşirme is not an instance of it.
> - **D.** Granting autonomy to minorities was what the Ottoman millet system often permitted communities that did not supply the sultan's soldiers. The devşirme removed people from their communities rather than granting those communities autonomy.
