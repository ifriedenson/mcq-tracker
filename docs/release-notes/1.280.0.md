# 1.280.0 — Cram sessions with 3-minute to 1-day ratings

## Review a deck now, on a compressed schedule

The Cards browse view now has a **Cram session** button beside the normal start button. It reviews every card in the current deck and course whether or not it is due, so a deck can be drilled without changing its ordinary schedule. The review header shows `Cram · 3m / 5m / 10m / 1d`, and the four rating buttons show their cram interval instead of the normal ladder preview.

### The four intervals

| Rating | Cram interval |
|---|---|
| Again | 3 minutes |
| Hard | 5 minutes |
| Good | 10 minutes |
| Easy | 1 day |

A cram rating writes the exact next due moment to the card's `next_review_at` frontmatter and keeps `next_review` as the `YYYY-MM-DD` date for compatibility. The Cards due queue, due counts and deck summaries compare the exact timestamp, so a card rated **Again** returns after three minutes rather than tomorrow. The field syncs through CouchDB with the other review properties.

### Normal scheduling is untouched

A normal (non-cram) rating deletes `next_review_at` and schedules exactly as before. Cram uses the same history, attempt id, offline outbox and note writes; it only replaces the due date for that rating.

### Limits

- **One pass at a time.** Cards do not requeue themselves while the view stays open; **Cram again** on the summary starts another full pass immediately rather than waiting for the stored 3/5/10-minute intervals.
- **100-card cap.** A cram session uses `cardQueue`'s existing maximum, so a view with more than 100 cards reviews the first 100 in queue order.
- **Cards only.** The cram intervals apply to the Cards review flow; question practice keeps its ladder/FSRS/SM-2 scheduling.
- The headless suite drives the UI through the fake DOM. The layout and live timing have not been checked in a real Obsidian window.
