# MCQ Tracker 1.182.0

Points of interest and a cursor trace in the Reference **Graph** tab.

## Points of interest

- Every explicit row (and every fitted regression row) marks its **roots**, its **y-intercept** and its local **extrema**. A crossing between two such rows is marked as an **intersection** on the later row.
- Roots come from a sign change between neighbouring samples, refined by bisection to machine precision, so `y = x^2 - 4` reports -2 and 2 rather than the nearest sample. Extrema come from a slope change and a ternary search, so `y = x^2 + 1` reports its vertex at 0 exactly.
- When several readings land on the same x they are merged: the vertex of `y = x^2 - 4` is one ring whose kinds are `intercept`, `root` and `minimum`.
- A tangency with no sign change is not reported as an intersection, and an identical pair of rows is skipped.
- At most 24 points are kept for a whole graph, so a busy window stays readable.
- A restriction already removed the samples it excludes, so `y = x^2 - 4 {x > 0}` reports only the root at 2.

## Cursor trace

- Hovering the canvas draws a dashed guide at the pointer's x and a dot on the nearest curve, and the readout under the canvas shows `x = … · y = … · row …`.
- The value is linearly interpolated between the two neighbouring samples, so it is a reading of the drawn curve rather than a re-evaluation of the expression.
- A gap under the cursor, such as `1 / x` at 0, reports `No curve at this x.` instead of a value, and leaving the canvas clears both the guide and the readout.
- Only explicit and fitted rows trace by x; polar, parametric, implicit, point and inequality rows are unchanged.

## Controls

- A **points of interest** checkbox next to the polar-grid control turns the rings and labels off and back on; it is on by default. The cursor trace stays available either way.

No new dependency, no `eval`, no `Function` and no network request: the solvers are local bisection and ternary search over the same compiled functions the canvas already draws.
