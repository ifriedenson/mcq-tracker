## Question

Which of the following imperial expansions was most similar to those of the Ottoman and Mughal Empires?

## Answer Choices

- A. The Portuguese Empire in Brazil
- B. The Dutch Empire in South Africa and Indonesia
- C. The Spanish Empire in the Americas and Asia
- D. The Manchu Empire in East Asia

## Answer

> [!answer]- Reveal Answer
> **Correct Answer: D**
>
> ### Rationale
>
> The Ottoman and Mughal empires both grew by contiguous overland conquest on the Eurasian steppe frontier, using cavalry and gunpowder artillery to absorb neighboring territory and subjects. The Manchu (Qing) conquest of East Asia followed the same pattern: a dynasty originating beyond the Great Wall in Manchuria overran Ming China and, later, Mongolia and Central Asia. All three were land-based empires built by territorial expansion, in contrast to the Portuguese, Dutch, and Spanish, whose empires were maritime, built on seaborne trade and coastal enclaves scattered across distant seas.
>
> **Why the other choices are wrong**
>
> - **A.** The Portuguese Empire in Brazil was a coastal, plantation-based colonial possession supplied by sea from Lisbon. It was not an overland expansion from a steppe-origin dynasty.
> - **B.** The Dutch presence in South Africa and Indonesia was a thin chain of fortified coastal trading posts dependent on shipping, not the contiguous territorial conquest that defined Ottoman and Mughal expansion.
> - **C.** The Spanish Empire was likewise oceanic — an American and Asian possession carried and administered across the Atlantic and Pacific, with Mexico and Peru as administrative centres rather than frontiers of continuous conquest.
