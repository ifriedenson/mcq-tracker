## Question

Which of the following states in the period 1450–1750 adopted a religious policy that was most different from the religious policy expressed in Source 2 ?

## Answer Choices

- A. The Mughal Empire under Akbar
- B. The Safavid Empire under Shah Ismail I
- C. The Ottoman Empire
- D. The Tokugawa Shogunate

## Answer

> [!answer]- Reveal Answer
> **Correct Answer: A**
>
> ### Rationale
>
> Source 2 expresses the drive for religious uniformity: a monarchy expelling a whole religious community on theological grounds. Akbar's policy was the diametrical opposite. He abandoned the earlier policy of religious suppression, abolished the jizya tax on non-Muslims, built temples for Hindus and Jainas, debated openly with scholars of every confession at the Ibadat Khana, and promoted a synthetic court religion, Din-i Ilahi, drawn from multiple traditions. Where Ferdinand and Isabella purified a single faith by expelling dissenters, Akbar governed a religiously plural population by inclusion.
>
> **Why the other choices are wrong**
>
> - **B.** Shah Ismail I established Twelver Shi'ism as the official confession of the Safavid state and persecuted Sunnis and other groups within it. This resembles the Spanish policy rather than differing from it.
> - **C.** The Ottoman Empire governed a highly diverse population through the millet system, giving religious communities recognised jurisdictions over their own affairs. That is a tolerant arrangement, but it is accommodation within a firmly Muslim state, which is much less of a departure from Source 2 than Akbar's sustained promotion of multiple faiths on equal terms.
> - **D.** The Tokugawa shogunate permitted Buddhism and regulated foreign religion, including suppressing Christianity after the Shimabara revolt. Its posture was controlled tolerance within a defined religious order rather than Akbar's active embrace of religious pluralism.
