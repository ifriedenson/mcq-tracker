## Question

Which of the following statements is true about both the Mughal and Ottoman empires in the sixteenth century?

## Answer Choices

- A. In both empires the majority of the people were Muslims.
- B. Both empires had powerful navies that engaged European navies.
- C. Both empires expanded through the use of gunpowder weapons and extensive bureaucracies.
- D. Both empires gave little monetary support to artistic and cultural endeavors.

## Answer

> [!answer]- Reveal Answer
> **Correct Answer: C**
>
> ### Rationale
>
> Both the Ottoman and the Mughal state were formed by conquest and then consolidated through a professional apparatus: artillery and musket infantry supplied by a revamped revenue system, and a large salaried bureaucracy drawn from diverse subject populations — the Ottomans through the devşirme palace schools and mansab service, the Mughals through the mansab-dawar system that tied office to revenue. That combination of gunpowder force and standing administration is the defining commonality of the two empires in the sixteenth century.
>
> **Why the other choices are wrong**
>
> - **A.** This is false for both. The Mughal population was overwhelmingly Hindu, with Muslims a substantial but smaller minority; the Ottoman population included large Orthodox Christian, Jewish, and other non-Muslim communities. Neither empire was in any sense predominantly Muslim.
> - **B.** Both were overwhelmingly continental. Neither maintained a navy of consequence; their rivalries were fought on land, and their interaction with European powers was largely diplomatic and commercial rather than naval.
> - **D.** False for both. Ottoman and Mughal courts were lavish patrons of manuscript illustration, tilework, calligraphy, and monumental architecture — the Süleymaniye complex and the Taj Mahal among the most famous examples of state-sponsored art in the period.
